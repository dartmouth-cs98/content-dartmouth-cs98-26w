You've built a grayscale prototype. Now it's time to validate it with real users and refine it into a polished, high-fidelity design.

Step 1: Get User Feedback

Before investing time in visual polish, make sure your prototype actually works for users. Test with 3-5 people outside your team — classmates from other teams, friends, or anyone in your target audience.

How to Run a Quick Test

  • Set a task, not a tour. Don't walk them through the app. Give them a goal: "You want to find a study group for CS50" or "You're trying to book a room for tomorrow."
  • Watch, don't help. Let them struggle. Where do they tap? Where do they hesitate? What do they say out loud?
  • Ask follow-up questions. "What did you expect to happen?" "What was confusing?" "What's missing?"

What to Look For

  • Confusion points — Where do users pause or look lost?
  • Wrong paths — Did they tap something expecting a different result?
  • Missing screens — Did they ask "what happens next?" or expect something that doesn't exist?
  • Language issues — Did they understand your labels and buttons?
Document your feedback. Create a "User Feedback" page in your Figma file. Note what you observed, what users said, and what you plan to change.

Step 2: Define Your Visual Direction

Now that the UX is validated, it's time to define how your product should feel. Create a Mood Board page in your Figma file.

Choose a UI Style

What personality fits your product and users?

  • Clean/Minimal — Lots of white space, simple typography, subtle colors
  • Friendly/Casual — Rounded corners, warm colors, playful elements
  • Professional/Formal — Sharp edges, muted palette, structured layouts
  • Bold/Edgy — High contrast, unconventional layouts, strong typography

Build Your Mood Board

  • Find 5-10 screenshots of apps or websites that capture the feeling you want
  • Note what you like about each — is it the color palette? The typography? The spacing?
  • Look for patterns across your inspiration

Step 3: Refine Based on Feedback

Address the usability issues you discovered in testing. Create a Mockups page in Figma and copy-paste your grayscale prototype (this preserves all your prototype connections).

  • Fix the flows — Rework any paths that confused users
  • Add missing states — Empty states, loading states, error messages, confirmation screens
  • Clarify the UI — Update labels, buttons, and copy that caused confusion
  • Simplify where needed — If users struggled, maybe there's too much on the screen

Step 4: Apply Visual Polish

Transform your grayscale prototype into high-fidelity mockups using your visual direction.

Color & Typography

  • Choose a primary color and 1-2 accent colors that fit your style
  • Pick a font pairing (one for headings, one for body — or just one versatile font)
  • Apply consistently across all screens

Visual Hierarchy

  • Hierarchy — Is it clear what's most important on each screen?
  • Alignment — Are elements lined up properly?
  • Spacing — Is there consistent padding and margins?
  • Touch targets — Are buttons big enough (at least 44x44pt)? Are they spaced apart from other tappable elements?

Consistency Check

  • Do similar elements look the same across screens?
  • Are your buttons, cards, and inputs styled consistently?
  • Does navigation work the same way throughout?

Step 5: Team Name & Logo

Create a Logo page in your Figma file. Your logo doesn't need to be complex — simple and clear beats elaborate and confusing.

  • There are 7 different types of logos — wordmarks and lettermarks are often easiest to create well
  • Try writing your team name in a distinctive font, then customize it:
    • Right-click the text → Outline Stroke to edit letter shapes
    • Adjust colors, add simple shapes, experiment

Design Is Ongoing

This assignment is your first pass at high-fidelity design — but design work continues throughout the project.

You'll get feedback from instructors, TAs, and classmates via Figma comments. Respond to all comments — either by making changes or explaining your reasoning. Resolve comments once you've addressed them.

As you build, you'll discover new edge cases and needed screens. Keep your Figma updated as the source of truth for how things should look.
