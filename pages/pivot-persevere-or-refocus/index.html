The Big Question

You spent a whole term building something. You talked to users, wrote code, tested features, and survived demo day. Nice work.

But here's the uncomfortable question: does your current direction actually deserve another 10 weeks of your time?

Today isn't about polishing your deck or tweaking your UI. It's about making a decision — with evidence — about whether to keep going, narrow down, or change course entirely. No hedging allowed.

🎯
By the end of class, every team will be able to say: who their user is, what evidence they actually have, whether they're pivoting/persevering/refocusing, and what they will test next.

How This Works

Here's the plan:

  1. Framing (this part, right now)
  2. Team Decision Brief + Slides (fill out the 5-part brief below, then make a few slides for shareout)
  3. Shareout (3-minute decisions from each team + class feedback)
  4. Wrap

The Decision Brief

This is the core of the activity. Your team fills out the 5 sections below in a FigJam board. Keep it tight — no fluff, no filler.

1. User + Problem

🚀 Write down:

  • Who exactly is your user? Not "students" or "people." A specific person with a specific need.
  • What painful problem are you solving? "Pain" means they're actively bothered by this, not just mildly inconvenienced.

2. What We Learned Last Term

🚀 List 2–3 concrete findings from actual users, testing, or usage data.

Not opinions. Not "we think." Things you observed, measured, or heard directly from users.

⚠️
If you can't list concrete findings, that's a finding in itself — it means you need more evidence before you can make a confident decision.

3. Biggest Risk Still Unresolved

🚀 What assumption could still kill this project?

Every project sits on a stack of assumptions. Some have been validated. Some haven't. Name the biggest one you still haven't tested.

4. Our Decision

🚀 Your team must commit to one of these. No hedging.

Persevere — same user + same problem + same direction. You have enough evidence that this problem is real for this user. Now iterate the solution.

Refocus — same problem, but you're narrowing the target user, use case, or feature set. The core idea is still alive, but you need to stop being broad. Honestly, this is where most teams end up — and that's fine.

Pivot — meaningful change in user, problem, or solution. Your evidence suggests you're solving the wrong problem, for the wrong user, or in the wrong way.

📌
"Refocus" is the underrated middle ground. A lot of teams don't need a dramatic pivot — they need to stop trying to be everything for everyone.

5. Next Test

🚀 Answer both:

  • What will you test in the next 7 days?
  • What result would prove you wrong?

That second question matters. If you can't define what "wrong" looks like, your test won't teach you anything.

Shareout

🚀 Put together a couple of slides to back up your shareout. Keep them simple — your decision brief sections map directly to slides:

  • Slide 1: User + problem + what you learned last term
  • Slide 2: 2 core screenshots of your product's current functionality
  • Slide 3: Your decision (persevere / refocus / pivot) + the evidence behind it
  • Slide 4: Biggest risk + next test (what you'll do this week and what would prove you wrong)

Each team gets 3 minutes. Use this structure:

"We originally thought [X]. After feedback, we're deciding to [persevere / refocus / pivot]. Our next test is [Z]."

After each team, the class responds:

Prompt 1: "Do you buy it?" — Hold up fingers: 1 = should pivot more, 2 = should narrow/refocus, 3 = should persevere.

Prompt 2: "Best next test?" — One person suggests the most important experiment the team should run.

Speak in Evidence

During this whole activity, you are banned from vague claims. Replace them with evidence.

⛔️ Don't say this✅ Say this instead
"people would use this""3 of 5 users said…"
"students need this""2 testers failed at…"
"we think""we observed…"
"our market is everyone""users asked for…"
"they liked it""nobody used [feature X]…"

If you can't finish the sentence with real data, that's your signal that you need to go get some.

Assumption Mapping

While you're working on the brief, take a few minutes to map your assumptions into 3 buckets:

Desirability (Do they want it?)

  • "This is a top-3 pain for our users"
  • "They'd actually try a new tool for this"
  • "They'd change their current behavior"

Feasibility (Can we build it?)

  • "We can integrate with X"
  • "The data we need actually exists"
  • "We can ship a stable core in 10 weeks"

Viability (Does it sustain?)

  • "Someone would pay for this" or "a partner would adopt it"
  • "There's a credible way to reach users"

🚀 Pick your top 2 riskiest assumptions — the ones most likely to kill the project if they turn out to be wrong. Those are what your next test should target.

Early Product-Market Fit (PMF) Signals

Here are 3 things you can actually measure this week to see if you're on track toward product-market fit:

  1. Would a user be meaningfully disappointed if this product didn't exist? (Ask them directly.)
  2. Will they take a real step to get value? Sign up, schedule a follow-up, upload data, invite a teammate — not just say "cool."
  3. Do they come back a second time for the intended workflow?

If the answer to all three is no, you're building something nobody needs yet. That's fixable — but only if you acknowledge it now.
